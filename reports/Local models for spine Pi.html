Recommendation

For the 2×16 GB target, run Qwen3.8-27B at 4-bit (RedHatAI/Qwen3.8-27B-INT4) on vLLM with tensor parallel 2, two Pi agents at 64K context, and point the LLM planner at the same server through /v1/messages. Keep Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4 on llama.cpp as the fast alternative and settle between them with the bake-off in section 6. Worker thinking stays off. The rule supervisor stays the default anywhere the planner would have to go through llama-server, because llama-server ignores output_config.

Update after the wider search (section 7). Two models change the picture, mainly because their KV cache is small:

  • Muse Glimmer 30B (Meta, dense, Apache-2.0) needs about 6.5 KiB of KV per token. On 2×16 GB at INT4 it fits 5 sessions at 64K on vLLM, against 2 for Qwen3.8-27B, with similar vendor scores (SWE-V 76.0 vs 77.2 for Qwen3.6-27B). Its thinking cannot be turned off, so each turn costs more tokens. It joins Qwen3.8-27B as a co-lead in the 2×16 bake-off, and it is the better fit at 24 GB (9 sessions at 64K against 1).
  • Mellum2.1 12B-A2.5B (JetBrains) replaces gpt-oss-20b at 16 GB: 10 sessions at 64K at Q4, and the only model with published results measured in Pi (SWE-V 47.0).
  • KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev, a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B fine-tune trained in a harness close to Pi's tools, replaces plain Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as the fast MoE alternative.

All three rest on vendor numbers only. The bake-off in section 6 decides.

Why these picks

  • Dense 27B over the MoE models where it fits. On the vendor's own minimal scaffolds, Qwen3.6-27B scores SWE-bench Verified 77.2 and Terminal-Bench 2.0 59.3, against 73.4 / 51.5 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and about 71 / 36.2 for Qwen3-Coder-Next vendor-reported. Qwen3.8-27B reports further gains over 3.6-27B (SWE-bench Pro 61.7 vs 53.5) vendor-reported. Sources: 3.6-27B card, 3.6-35B-A3B card, Coder-Next tech report, 3.8-27B card.
  • The MoE alternative is about speed. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B has 3B active parameters, so it decodes much faster than a dense 27B and fits 3 sessions at 64K on 2×16 GB. The only independent Pi-based number is roughly half the vendor TB2 score (23.8% mean with little-coder) independent, which is why the bake-off decides.
  • gpt-oss-20b is weak at agentic terminal work. It scores 3.1% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with Terminus-2 (arXiv 2602.21193) independent. It is in the plan only because it is the one model that leaves room for 2 sessions on 16 GB.
  • Planner on the worker's server works only on vLLM ≥0.23.0 or SGLang ≥0.5.14, which map output_config.format to JSON-schema decoding (vLLM PR #42396, SGLang PR #25876). llama-server drops output_config (PR #17570 converter), so the workaround there needs a Spine code change.

Verdicts on the four doubts

These were raised in an earlier review. Two hold, two needed correction.

1 · Confirmed

gpt-oss-20b size and reasoning

MoE with 20.9B total and 3.6B active (model card Table 1). The native MXFP4 checkpoint is 13.76 GB (12.8 GiB). Every GGUF quant lands at about 11.5–12.1 GB because the MXFP4 expert tensors are kept. It is a reasoning model that must use the harmony format, and reasoning cannot be turned off: low is the minimum.

Sources: HF card, model card arXiv 2508.10925, harmony guide, unsloth GGUF.

2 · Corrected

Dense 27B FP8 at 64–128K on 32 GB

Does not fit. The weights alone are 30.87 GB (Qwen3.8-27B-FP8), above the 24.94 GB usable on 2×16 GB and the 27.94 GB usable on a single 32 GB card computed. With the planned 4-bit weights on 2×16 GB TP: INT4 (19.45 GB) gives 4 / 2 / 1 sessions at 32K / 64K / 128K; UD-Q4_K_XL GGUF (17.56 GB) gives 6 / 3 / 1, but llama.cpp splits by layer, which is not true tensor parallelism.

3 · Correct only at BF16

Qwen3-Coder-Next about 3.2 GB per 128K session

That figure is BF16 KV: 12 full-attention layers × 2 KV heads × 256 head dim × 2 (K+V) × 2 B × 131,072 = 3.22 GB. With FP8 KV it is 1.61 GB plus about 77 MB of DeltaNet state computed. The bigger issue is the weights: 4-bit Coder-Next is about 48–50 GB (AWQ 48.25 GB, UD-Q4_K_XL 49.61 GB), so it only fits the 64 GB tier.

4 · Corrected

Pi prompt size

It is well above 150 words. Pi 1.5.0's system prompt is about 2,284 characters, 289 words, ≈570 tokens. The four tool schemas add ≈710 tokens. With the chat-template wrapper that is ≈1.3–1.6K tokens before the first user message, plus any AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md found in the worktree or its parents, added verbatim. Spine does not pass --no-context-files. Reconstructed from source at tag v1.5.0, not captured on the wire.

Source: Pi v1.5.0 packages/coding-agent/src/core/system-prompt.ts, tool files under core/tools/.

Notes that change how the numbers are used.

  • On the Qwen hybrids, --kv-cache-dtype fp8 only touches the full-attention layers (one in four). The recurrent state is set by --mamba-ssm-cache-dtype. The table already counts only the full layers.
  • FP8 KV on Hopper lost long-context accuracy until two-level accumulation became the default (128K NIAH 91% → 13% → 89%). Prefill can be up to ~1.6× slower at head_dim 256, which is the Qwen3.5/3.6/3.8 attention head size (vLLM FP8 KV blog).
  • For gpt-oss, keep the sliding-window layers native: --kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers sliding_window (quantized KV doc).
  • GLM-4.7-Flash's number assumes an MLA latent cache. A runtime that expands K/V needs 18× more.

Pi 1.5.0 compat for local URLs

Pi auto-detects provider quirks by substring on baseUrl. Nothing matches localhost, a private IP, vLLM, SGLang or llama.cpp, so a local server gets the "real OpenAI" profile: store:false, the developer role for reasoning models, max_completion_tokens, and thinkingFormat:"openai". Set compat explicitly. Pi's built-in llama.cpp provider sets exactly this, but Spine's --no-extensions disables it.

Thinking

  • Spine passes no --thinking, so a reasoning:true model runs at Pi's default medium unless settings.json or adapter args override it.
  • reasoning:false sends no thinking field, so a Qwen hybrid still thinks by template default. Use reasoning:true with qwen-chat-template and level off.
  • Override order: --thinking / --model p/id:level, then settings.json modelThinkingLevels["provider/id"], then defaultThinkingLevel, then medium.
  • --thinking off is a real Pi flag (CA/docs/cli.md), so adapter args: ["--thinking", "off"] works as an alternative to settings.json.
  • gpt-oss: map off and minimal to "low" in thinkingLevelMap; Pi then sends reasoning_effort:"low".
  • Spine copies settings.json into the container's private agent dir, so compaction.modelOverrides, modelThinkingLevels, defaultThinkingLevel and thinkingBudgets reach Pi without code changes.

Compaction

Defaults are reserveTokens 16384 and keepRecentTokens 20000. At 32K compaction triggers at 16,384 tokens but tries to keep 20K, so it cannot work. Per-model overrides in settings.json:

5. Fit to Spine

How Spine settings map to server capacity

  • max_agents is the binding cap. The multi-repo supervisor (supervisor.yaml in $SPINE_HOME or ~/.config/spine) defaults to max_agents: 4, max_gates: 2. Each repo's .spine/config.yaml parallel defaults to 4. Three repos at parallel: 4 ask for 12 runs, but only max_agents run at once.
  • Set max_agents ≤ the sessions the server holds at the chosen context (section 2).
  • Set --max-num-seqs (vLLM) or --parallel (llama-server) = max_agents, plus 1 when the planner shares the server.
  • Over-subscription does not fail fast. Extra requests queue (or get preempted), and a long prefill behind them can trip stall_timeout.
  • Per-repo parallel can stay at its default as long as max_agents is right. Lower it if one repo should not take every slot.

Container network

container.network defaults to host. That lets the worker reach the model server on 127.0.0.1, and it also lets the worker reach every other port on the host and the LAN, including Spine's own API on 127.0.0.1:8765. Options, all with the existing network setting:

  1. User-defined Docker network (preferred). docker network create spine-models, run the model server as a container on it (for example named llm), set container.network: spine-models and baseUrl: http://llm:8000/v1. The worker sees only that network. Host-side tools that use the same models.json then need the server's port published too.
  2. Dedicated address. Bind the server to an address on a bridge the containers use (the Docker bridge gateway, or a dummy interface), point baseUrl there and use that network instead of host.
  3. Keep host and firewall. Add host firewall rules that limit what the container uid can reach. This is the least isolated and the easiest to get wrong.

Planner (LLM supervisor) on a local server

  • The planner is Spine's LLM supervisor: supervisor: {type: llm, model, effort, timeout} in .spine/config.yaml. The default is type: rules.
  • It calls /v1/messages through the Go Anthropic SDK with output_config.format (JSON schema) and effort, using ANTHROPIC_API_KEY. To reach a local server, set ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL in the controller's environment. That the Go SDK honours this variable is an assumption to check unverified.
  • vLLM ≥0.23.0 (PR #42396) and SGLang ≥0.5.14 (PR #25876) map output_config.format to JSON-schema decoding and effort to reasoning_effort. vLLM applies the grammar after the reasoning section, so thinking can stay on.
  • llama-server ignores output_config and effort. Its documented extra_params object would work (option.WithJSONSet("extra_params", ...)), but that is a Spine code change. Keep the rule supervisor on llama.cpp tiers.
  • Thinking on for planner requests: Qwen3.8's template thinks by default and the planner sends no enable_thinking:false. Whether vLLM's reasoning_effort reaches Qwen3.8's template effort levels is unverified.

Quality and Costs pages: scope (no code now)

The dashboard on main has overview, task, events and create routes. There were no Quality or Costs pages on main when checked; they are being built in another session. This scope is an addition to those pages.

api.Outcome today records attempt, round, reason, stop_reason, exit_code, tokens_used, cost_usd, turns and duration. It has no adapter, no model and no tool-error counts. The task Spec has adapter (empty means the config default).

  1. Record identity. Add adapter name and resolved provider/model to each Outcome, and to the decision/outcome event. Resolve the empty Spec adapter to the config default at run time.
  2. Count tool errors. In pirpc.go, count tool_execution_end events with isError per tool name (edit, bash, read, write), and count text-leaked tool calls from assistant text.
  3. Quality page. Group by adapter + model, with a filter: first-round pass rate, rounds per task, aborts by reason, failed edits per run.
  4. Costs page. Same grouping and filter: tokens, turns, duration. cost_usd is 0 for local models, so show tokens and duration as the primary cost.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every new Outcome has non-empty adapter and model; the decision/outcome event carries both.
  • A run with a failing edit shows tool_errors.edit ≥ 1; a clean run shows none.
  • Quality and Costs pages group by adapter + model and filter to one model; numbers match a manual count over the stored outcomes for at least one bake-off candidate.
  • Older outcomes without the new fields render as "unknown" and do not break either page.

Estimate: a small change, touching api.Outcome, internal/agent/pirpc.go, the storage events and the two dashboard pages.

Serving notes for the new candidates

  • Muse Glimmer: vLLM --tool-call-parser muse_glimmer --reasoning-parser muse_glimmer --generation-config auto, image v0.28.0 (vLLM recipe); SGLang cookbook exists; llama.cpp b10353+ with --jinja. Thinking always runs: set reasoning strength low and cap it with --reasoning-budget on llama.cpp. Expect more tokens per turn than a thinking-off Qwen.
  • Mellum2.1: vLLM hermes tool parser, qwen3 reasoning parser; official GGUF with an MTP draft head for speculative decoding.
  • KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev: identical to Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (qwen3_coder / qwen3, --language-model-only on vLLM). No MTP head. No unsloth GGUF; use bartowski.
  • Laguna XS 2.1: vLLM poolside_v1 parsers need a build with PR #47311, otherwise tool calls are dropped silently. llama.cpp support merged in PR #25165, with open reports of reasoning running to the token budget.
  • North Mini Code: vLLM cohere_command4 parsers plus the cohere_melody package. In llama.cpp use the repo's chat_template.jinja; the older template leaks reasoning into the reply.

Checked and set aside

  • Too big for 64 GB at 4-bit: gpt-oss-120b (65.25 GB MXFP4), Mistral Small 4 (74 GB at Q4), every current DeepSeek, MiniMax, MiMo, StepFun, Tencent Hy3 and GLM-5.x model (199B and up). Zhipu has no GLM-4.6/4.7-Air.
  • Pruned or very low-bit large MoEs: MiniMax-M2.5-REAP-139B at IQ3_XXS (53.6 GB) leaves no room for even one 32K session at 124 KiB/token; GLM-4.5-Air-REAP-82B at IQ4_XS fits 1 at 64K. The REAP authors' own results show multi-turn tool calling can collapse at 50% pruning (Kimi-K2 BFCLv3 0.355 to 0.164), and a community audit found about 60% of 50%-pruned releases failed (REAP paper, reap-eval). The 50%-pruned KAT-Coder reports SWE-V 52.0 against 69.4 unpruned. Not recommended for Pi.
  • Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B claims SWE-V 79 and TB 2.1 67.8, but Kwaipilot's re-run of Ornith-1.0 scored 55.8 / 36.0 against claims of 75.6 / 64.2 (KAT card). Same architecture as KAT-Coder; include only if a slot is free.
  • Dense models with full attention everywhere (Seed-OSS-36B, KAT-Dev-72B-Exp, Granite 4.2) fit very few sessions; Seed-OSS gets 2 at 64K on 48 GB.
  • 2025 SWE fine-tunes (OpenHands-LM, SWE-agent-LM, Skywork-SWE, DeepSWE, SERA, Kimi-Dev-72B and others): 32–40K context or heavy KV, scores at or below the Qwen3.6 bases, and they learned OpenHands or SWE-agent tool formats. SERA's card warns it may call a nonexistent submit tool.
  • License exclusions: Hunyuan-A13B excludes the EU, UK and South Korea; openPangu forbids EU use; CWM is non-commercial; EXAONE, Solar, Falcon and Llama 4 are non-permissive.
  • Serving not ready: LongCat-Flash-Lite-Sparse (SWE-V 68.2 vendor) is not in vLLM or SGLang main, and its 4-bit builds are 84–89 GB. The pruned Qwen3.8-Flash-Next coder GGUF (29.6 GB on GPU) is experimental and llama.cpp-only.
